In Defense of the Poor Image (by Hito Steyerl, 2011)

I found Hito Steyerl's "Poor Images" (2011) really revealing in understanding the complexity around "the image" in the context of the world today. With rapid advances in technology and social media, images circulate differently; networks, compression, and platforms change how we see and value images. (Content shortened for this page.)

Within the essay, she does not present the "poor image" as a perfect alternative to commercial media or capitalism. She highlights the contradiction that the poor image has the ability to resist commercial circulation while at the same time being deeply entangled in the very systems it seeks to critique. The essay prompted me to think about authorship, authority, and how degraded or widely circulated images carry their own histories.

Reading her essay made me think of a work I saw this year in Venice by artist Yuyan Wang called "Boring Billion." The single-channel video was composed purely from found footage online. What was interesting about the piece was how the recontextualization of widely circulated, low-resolution clips created a new narrative and emotional register. It demonstrated how the "poor image" can be repurposed in contemporary art practices.
