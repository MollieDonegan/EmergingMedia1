Week 6

I really enjoyed reading The Electronic Disturbance, particularly the discussion around public space, ownership and the ways in which power operates through the spaces we inhabit. It made me think about how much our relationship with public space has changed, and how the places where we meet, spend time and connect with one another are not always as open or accessible as we might think.

One of the ideas that really stood out to me was the authors' discussion of the decline of the street and the movement towards privatised public spaces, such as shopping centres. They describe how public spaces are increasingly presented as unsafe or undesirable, while privately owned spaces offer a sense of safety, comfort and familiarity. However, this comes with conditions. You are welcome to occupy these spaces as long as you follow certain expectations, consume and behave in ways that are considered acceptable.

I found this particularly interesting when thinking about class and who actually gets to use public space. There is a difference between having access to a space and feeling that you are welcome to be there. Public space should offer opportunities for people to come together, rather than being organised entirely around consumption or productivity.

This reading felt useful to me for my art practice. I am interested in socially engaged methodologies and working with people through shared experiences. I am interested in how art can create opportunities for people to come together who might not otherwise meet, and how making, playing, talking or simply spending time together can become ways of building relationships. I often think about how art can act as a bridge into spaces, making them feel more welcoming and giving people a reason to enter, participate and feel that they have a place there.

For me, this is also about thinking beyond the traditional idea of an art space. A gallery or cultural institution can sometimes feel quite intimidating or inaccessible, particularly for people who have not grown up feeling that these spaces are for them. I am interested in what happens when artistic practice moves outside of these settings and into everyday public spaces, where people already live, gather and spend time. Could socially engaged art create different ways of occupying these spaces? Could it help people feel a greater sense of ownership, connection and belonging?

The reading made me think deeper on how much our relationships with physical space have shifted alongside the growth of social media. I feel that social media has increasingly taken over many of the ways we communicate and maintain relationships. So much of our social interaction now happens through screens like talking about a bad day at work with someone close. While these platforms are accessible in some sense and make it easier to stay connected across distances, I do wonder what we lose when so much of our communication becomes digital. Has the way we relate to one another, and even to ourselves, fundamentally changed?

I think there is something important about physically showing up somewhere or for someone and being present and sharing an experience that cannot be completely replicated online. Meeting a friend, sitting together, walking through a neighbourhood or unexpectedly finding yourself in conversation with someone are all small but meaningful parts of social life. These interactions are not always planned or “productive,” but they help create a sense of connection to other people and to the places we inhabit.

What I found interesting about the reading is that it does not suggest that physical forms of resistance are no longer important, but rather that they need to be considered alongside the growing power of electronic networks. I agree that we need to think critically about the digital spaces we now occupy. However, I also think it is important not to lose sight of the physical spaces around us and the possibilities they hold for embodied collective experience.

The reading left me thinking about what it means to share space with other people and what this might look like in the future. I fear for our relationships, exchange ideas, memories and sense of belonging as we move towards a techno individual way of existing as people.
