In Defense of the Poor Image

by Hito Steyerl, 2011

I found Hito Steyerl's Poor Images (2011) piece really revealing in understanding the complexity around 'the image," in the context of the world today. With rapid advances in technology and society evolving at such a rapid pace, the concepts Steyerl discusses remain remarkably relevant.

Within the essay, she does not present 'the poor image," as a perfect alternative to commercial media or capitalism. She highlights the contradiction that the poor image has the ability to resist conventional structures while still existing within them, creating a nuanced perspective on how images function in our current digital landscape.

Reading her essay made me think of a work I saw this year in Venice by artist Yuyan Wang called Boring Billion. The single channel video was composed purely from found footage online. What was once disparate digital content becomes unified through creative recontextualization, much like the poor images Steyerl describes—existing in multiple forms across platforms, constantly circulated and reinterpreted.
