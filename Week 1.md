# In Defense of the Poor Image (by Hito Steyerl, 2011)

I found Hito Steyerl's Poor Images (2011) piece really revealing in understanding the complexity around 'the image," in the context of the world today. With rapid advances in technology and society evolving at breakneck speed, the traditional understanding of images has become increasingly complicated and multifaceted.

Within the essay, she does not present 'the poor image," as a perfect alternative to commercial media or capitalism. She highlights the contradiction that the poor image has the ability to resist commodification while simultaneously being a product of the same systems it seeks to oppose. This nuance is important because it acknowledges the reality of our media landscape without offering false hope.

Reading her essay made me think of a work I saw this year in Venice by artist Yuyan Wang called Boring Billion. The single channel video was composed purely from found footage online. What was once considered disposable material—low-resolution clips, screen recordings, and discarded digital fragments—was repurposed into something profound and worthy of gallery space.
