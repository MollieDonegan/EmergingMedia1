# In Defense of the Poor Image (by Hito Steyerl, 2011)

I found Hito Steyerl's Poor Images (2011) piece really revealing in understanding the complexity around 'the image," in the context of the world today. With rapid advances in technology and society evolving rapidly, her analysis of how images are reproduced, shared, and circulated feels increasingly relevant.

Within the essay, she does not present 'the poor image," as a perfect alternative to commercial media or capitalism. She highlights the contradiction that the poor image has the ability to resist commercial homogenization while simultaneously being complicit in the very systems it critiques. This nuance is important and prevents the essay from falling into naive utopianism.

Reading her essay made me think of a work I saw this year in Venice by artist Yuyan Wang called Boring Billion. The single channel video was composed purely from found footage online. What was once considered low-quality or disposable digital material became the raw material for artistic expression, embodying many of the ideas Steyerl discusses about the redemptive potential of poor images.
