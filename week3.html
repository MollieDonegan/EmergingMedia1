In Bush’s text, he explores how technology could be used to support and extend human thinking. He has a very positive outlook on technological development, particularly in relation to how science has increased our quality of life, knowledge, communication and access to information. He writes about the “memex”, a device designed to store “records and communications”. What he is describing is an early idea of personal computing and the way modern technology can organise and connect information. He describes the memex as “an enlarged intimate supplement to his memory.”

One part of the text that I felt related to our coding project was Bush’s argument that “the human mind operates by association.” We do not necessarily think in a completely straight line. One thought can lead to another, and we make connections between different pieces of information. This made me think about the way I have been building my text-based game. Trying to create links between text, information, images and sound, allowing the user(classmates) to move from one piece of information to another.Bush writes that “the process of tying two items together is the important thing.” Much like HTML coding and the structure of my game. One page needs to connect to another page for the game to function and make sense. The player makes a choice, which then sends them along a different path or “trail”. In this way, the game is not completely linear. The player creates their own route through the information based on the choices they make.

Bush was writing about these ideas in 1945, long before the internet and HTML coding which is crazy! Reading it while actually struggling through the process of making a digital project made me understand his ideas in a more practical way. The links in my game are quite simple, but they are still about creating relationships between different pieces of information and allowing someone to navigate between them.
